## 1. Units, representative, and original identity flow

- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF pp. 6-8, Secs. 2.1, 3.1, Algorithm 1): after modulus raising, a coefficient decrypts to `q0 I + Delta m` modulo the raised ciphertext modulus. `I` is an integer introduced by lifting the `q0` residue; `m` is the encoded message. Cleaning removes `I`, leaving the original encoded `Delta m`. Treating the raised coefficient as `I + m/g` uses `g = q0/Delta` and requires division by the **actual** `q0`, not a metadata-only change of scale. `I` is fixed by the input ciphertext and lift convention; the two subtraction branches must use the same representative.
- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF p. 7, Algorithm 1): the CtS-first form is `ct=ModRaise(ctin)`, `ct'=StC(EvalRound(CtS#(ct)))`, `ctout=ct-ct'`. `CtS#` intentionally has low plaintext precision; final StC has high precision. The paper replaces `EvalRound` with `Combine o VecClean o DigitExtract` (PDF pp. 7-8, Definitions 3.1-3.3, Theorem 3.4).
- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF p. 8, Sec. 3.1; p. 12, Sec. 5.2): reported configurations use StC-first EvalRound+. Apply high-precision StC **before** ModRaise. After raising, fork the same coefficient ciphertext: `CtS# -> EvalRound` yields the accurate slot-encoded `I`; high-precision CtS yields slot-encoded `I + m/g`; subtract and scale by `g` to obtain `m`. The high-precision bypass CtS is a necessary second transform, not a replacement for `CtS#`. The paper's coefficient-count issue requires real/imaginary extraction, parallel rounding, and recombination because there are `N` real coefficients but `N/2` complex slots (PDF pp. 6-7). Exact subtraction scale, level, and key alignment must be established in a concrete backend.
- **DERIVED** (from PDF p. 12, Sec. 5.2): if high CtS is `I+m/g+eps0` and cleaned integer is `I+eps1`, then result before final normalization is `m/g+eps0-eps1`; after multiplying by `g`, its error is `g(eps0-eps1)`. Thus the errors of **both** branches must be bounded at the subtraction point. A correct integer cleaner alone does not certify the bootstrap.

## 2. DigitExtract: targets and coefficient construction

- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF p. 7, Definition 3.1): with base `beta`, `kappa` digits, initial integer error `epsilon in (0,1/8]`, each extracted digit must be within `epsilon_de in (2 epsilon,1/4]` of the intended digit, including homomorphic evaluation error. The paper permits adding `K` to make a signed `I` nonnegative. For its implementation it extracts five bits on the 32-center domain `I in [-16,15]` (PDF p. 11, Sec. 5.2). The approximation domain is a union of small intervals around these integer centers, **not** a continuous interval across bit transitions (PDF p. 8, Sec. 3.2; p. 11, Sec. 5.2).
- **DERIVED / IMPLEMENTATION CHOICE**: define `u=I+16 in [0,31]`, `b_j(I)=floor(u/2^j) mod 2` for `j=0,...,4`, and reconstruct `I=sum_j 2^j b_j-16`. This resolves signed endpoints exactly. To construct direct polynomial coefficients, choose a certified input radius `r` with `0<r<1/8` and `r >= sup |m/g+eps_CtS#|` over the declared message and transform domain. Let `U_r=union_{n=-16}^{15}[n-r,n+r]`. Fit five real polynomials `P_j` by minimax/multi-interval Remez so `max_{x in U_r}|P_j(x)-b_j(n(x))| <= epsilon_poly,j`; then include coefficient quantization and encrypted evaluation error in `epsilon_de,j`. This is the paper-sanctioned *method*, not its original unpublished coefficient set. Keep the interval endpoints, precision, minimax residual/certificate, coefficient format, evaluator graph, and test grid with any generated profile. Independent interval bounds are needed beyond sampled-grid results.
- **DERIVED / IMPLEMENTATION CHOICE**: point interpolation alone has a reproducible formula, `L_n(x)=product_{m=-16,m!=n}^{15}(x-m)/(n-m)` and `P_j^point(x)=sum_n b_j(n)L_n(x)` (degree at most 31). It is exact at the centers but offers no interval guarantee and can have large derivatives. The paper explicitly warns of that instability (PDF p. 8, Sec. 3.2); this formula is only a reference oracle, not an acceptable substitute for the fitted interval polynomials.
- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF pp. 11-12, Sec. 5.2): their direct path uses **five distinct degree-47 polynomials**, a shared odd basis through degree 15, Chebyshev `T2,T4,T8,T16,T32`, and a decomposition `p=p0+T16 p1+T32 p2`. It reports 22 non-scalar multiplications total and depth 6. The paper supplies neither five coefficient arrays nor the exact Remez interval widths, fitting weights, variable normalization, output coding, per-stage scales, or evaluator graph. **UNRESOLVED**: these values cannot be represented as the authors' exact implementation from this PDF.
- **DERIVED, NOT PAPER-PRESCRIBED**: the odd-basis claim is compatible with centering `x=(I+1/2)/16` and odd targets `s_j(I)=2b_j(I)-1`, since `s_j(-I-1)=-s_j(I)`. Then fit odd degree-47 `S_j(x)=sum_{k=0}^{23}a_{j,k}T_{2k+1}(x)` on the correspondingly shifted intervals, and output `(1+S_j)/2`. This is one concrete coefficient-generation convention. Neither the `+1/2`, division by 16, nor the `+1` output offset is stated by the paper; verify this convention and the claimed evaluation count independently before calling it a reproduction. It must cover both `I=-16` and `I=15` and all 32 intervals.
- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF pp. 8, 11-12, Secs. 3.2, 5.2): the alternate indirect path approximates `exp(2 pi i I/2^kappa)` by a degree-8 polynomial on scaled input `[-pi,pi]`, then uses sparse root-of-unity digit extraction. Its least-significant-bit example is `(1-x^(2^(kappa-1)))/2`. The reported degree-8 approximation has about 12 bits of accuracy and depth 3 using Grafting; the whole indirect extraction uses more depth than the direct path. No numeric exponential coefficients are supplied in this paper either.

## 3. VecClean, trits, and recombination

- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF p. 6, Lemma 2.1; p. 8, Sec. 3.2): for `b in {0,1}`, `h1(x)=3x^2-2x^3`, `h1(b)=b`, `h1'(b)=0`, and `|h1(b+e)-b|<=5|e|^2` for `|e|<=1`. Iterate independently for each extracted bit; each iteration is two multiplicative levels in a conventional evaluation, while scale/precision may double.
- **DERIVED**: the ideal uniform recurrence is `E_(r+1)<=5 E_r^2`; encrypted execution needs `E_(r+1)<=5 E_r^2+eta_r`, where `eta_r` bounds that iteration's evaluation, key-switch, rescale, and coefficient errors after decoding. Apply the bound only while its input remains within the lemma's domain. This recurrence is conservative; `5E^2` alone does not prove improvement at the allowed `epsilon_de=1/4`. At `E=1/4` the bound is `5/16`; convergence must be checked with actual tighter bounds or a direct interval analysis. Do not infer 81 bits from three iterations without the initial error and `eta_r` ledger.
- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF pp. 7-8, Definitions 3.2-3.3, Theorem 3.4): `VecClean` must preserve each digit and deliver error at most `epsilon_clean<=1/4`; `Combine` produces error at most `2 beta^(kappa-1) epsilon_clean` in the paper's generic bound. Correctness requires that bound to be at most the `EvalRound` target `epsilon'`. For the chosen exact real bit representation, **DERIVED** sharper bound is `|e_I|<=sum_j 2^j E_j` and, with common `E`, `|e_I|<=31E`; restore the signed offset `-16` exactly. The paper says integer-weighted recombination needs no rescale/modulus consumption (PDF p. 8, Sec. 3.2); backend integer multiplications still need adequate ciphertext modulus and scale tracking.
- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF pp. 14-15, Appendix A, Lemma A.1, Theorem A.2, Figure 4, Tables 3-4): trits are represented as cube roots `omega^d`, not real digits during cleaning. For `x` near `omega^d`, `y` near `conj(omega^d)`, `f3(x,y)=(y^2+4x-2x^2 y)/3`, and `|f3(x,y)-omega^d|<=3 max(|e_x|,|e_y|)^2` for input errors at most 1. Thus the ideal recurrence is `E_(r+1)<=3 E_r^2`; encrypted execution adds a quantified `eta_r`, including conjugation. Figure 4 evaluates the integer numerator with conjugation, tensor, relinearization, and final rescale, hiding division by 3 in the scale; it does **not** make conjugation error free. Reconstruction extracts the imaginary part of roots corresponding to trits `-1,0,1` and linearly weights by powers of 3. Table 4's 27-period alternative covers `[-13,13]` with reported `2^-49` failure probability, unlike the five-bit 32-period zero-failure setup. The appendix gives sparse *shapes* of trit extraction polynomials for `k=3` but not their `alpha_i` coefficients.

## 4. Thrifty arithmetic, Grafting, and scale/domain ledger

- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF pp. 9-10, Sec. 4.1, Figure 2, Theorems 4.1-4.2): for an input scale `D`, evaluate `c2=Relin(c tensor c)`, `c3=Relin(c tensor c2)`, `c4=3D*c2-2*c3`, `out=Rescale_D(c4)`. Both tensors precede the one rescale; output scale is `D^2`, while modulus loss is a factor `D`, compared with `D^4` for the black-box circuit. After `k` ideal steps the scale is `D^(2^k)` and total loss `D^(2^k-1)`. Tensor is exact as algebra on the current decrypted value, while relinearization and rescale add errors. This is a mathematical operation sequence; a library's multiply-and-rescale API may not implement it.
- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF pp. 10-11, Sec. 4.1): practical precision lags log scale by a gap `c` (example `c ~ 12` bits). After a cleaning, optionally rescale further to avoid doubling this gap unnecessarily. The integer multiplication by `D` in Figure 2 presumes an integer scale. For noninteger `D`, the paper chooses `D'=round(D)` and reinterprets the input as `z'=(D/D')z`; this introduces a representation error that must enter the ledger. Appendix A, Figure 4 supplies the corresponding thrifty trit sequence with conjugation and the `1/3` factor absorbed into final scaling.
- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF pp. 6, 11, Secs. 2.2, 5.1): Grafting decouples CKKS scale from RNS modulus factors. Rescale may switch from `Q_a` to a nondividing `Q_b`, and the *actual* ratio and resulting scale must be tracked; high-precision encoding/scale arithmetic used quadruple precision (`libquadmath`) in the authors' HEaaN implementation. **UNRESOLVED** for Cheddar: whether its native rescale/basis conversion has these semantics and sufficient scale precision; matching prime bit sizes or decrementing a level number is not evidence of Grafting.
- **PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF pp. 11-12, Sec. 5.2): `g=q0/Delta` where `Delta` is the **StC output coefficient scale** in the StC-first setup. The normalized raised coefficient is `I+m/g`; the high-CtS bypass result is `I+m/g+eps0`. The erroneous integer's initial precision is controlled by `g` together with StC and CtS errors. The most-significant bit loses about 4 bits in their setup; after `n` cleaning iterations the paper gives the rough output-precision ceiling `2^n(log2 g-4)-log2 g` bits. It is a heuristic, not a parameter proof. For target plaintext precision `t`, scale bits should satisfy `log2 Delta >=~ t+delta`, `delta ~ log2 sqrt(Nh)`; their discussion gives cleaning-scale growth from about `2^(t+delta)` to `2^(2t+delta)` at about `t+2delta` bits of modulus use per such step. Numerical scale and modulus consumption depend on the actual circuit.
- **DERIVED / IMPLEMENTATION CHOICE**: a reproducible ledger must record for each branch the modulus `Q`, integer representative, decoded plaintext unit, actual scale (not only bit count), worst-case error, ciphertext components, and consumed modulus. Required domain checks are `I in [-16,15]`, `|m/g+eps_CtS#|<=r<1/8`, each fitted interval within its certified range, and exact scale/level alignment before subtraction. The zero-failure statement below is conditional on the paper's secret distribution and domain proof; it does not transfer automatically to a new `q0`, secret, or MEBOOT entry.

## 5. Reported configurations and limits of reproduction

**PAPER-EXPLICIT** (PDF p. 11, Sec. 5; p. 13, Sec. 5.3, Table 2): CPU HEaaN/Grafting, `N=2^16`, sparse ternary secret Hamming weight `h=30`, key-switch gadget rank `dnum=6`, maximum `PQ` 1533 bits, Table 2 usable budget 1289 bits. CPU Apple M4 Pro, one thread, at least 20 repetitions. Precision is `-log2(max observed decrypted error)`, not a worst-case theorem. `h=30` yields their claimed 32-center domain bound `||(b+as)/q0||_infinity <=(h+1)/2=15.5`, hence zero failure **for that approximation domain under those assumptions** (PDF p. 11, Sec. 5.2). Their 128-bit RLWE estimate and broader security aspirations do not prove a different implementation's security.

## 6. Original baseline versus MEBOOT candidate

- **IMPLEMENTATION CHOICE - original baseline**: first instantiate the five-bit 32-center direct path, or the paper's separately identified indirect path, under an independently generated and certified coefficient profile. Preserve StC-first, the low-precision integer-cleaning branch, the high-precision CtS bypass, subtraction in common units, and paper-consistent domain and scale checks. Prove plaintext polynomial behavior before encrypted validation. Call it a *paper-derived implementation* until the missing author constants and end-to-end outputs are independently matched; no PDF formula alone establishes a faithful runtime reproduction.
- **IMPLEMENTATION CHOICE - later MEBOOT candidate**: MEBOOT's low-`q0P` entry, four-role CtS, larger secret/domain, and six-bit extraction require a separate profile, coefficient fit, bounds, security calculation, and baseline comparison. They are not parameters or claims of this CCS paper. In particular, six extracted bits do not specify multiplicative depth, and the five-bit interval fit cannot simply be reused for a 64-center domain. The original branch and its evidence should remain independently selectable.
- **UNRESOLVED - original code provenance**: the paper (PDF p. 11, Sec. 5) says the experiments were developed on C++ HEaaN but gives no source repository, coefficient file, or release identifier. The [first author's publication page](https://hmchoe0528.github.io/publications.html?lang=eng) lists paper, preprint, and blog for this publication, but no GitHub link; its GitHub links belong to *other* publications. Targeted searches found no verifiable author repository for this implementation. HEaaN itself and the [author's explanatory blog](https://ckks.org/blog/2025/high_prec_bootstrap/) are secondary context, not provenance for the missing polynomial coefficients. Do not import a third-party coefficient set as original without a traceable author artifact and hash.
